										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<p><strong>Introduction </strong></p>
<p><strong>The phenomenon of forced disappearances in Indian Illegally Occupied Jammu and Kashmir (IIOJK) since 1989 has resulted in a new social category – the half widow – a woman whose husband has disappeared without any legal declaration of death.</strong> <strong>The number of these women is estimated at about 1,500<a href="#_ftn1" name="_ftnref1">[1]</a> but they live in a condition of legal and existential vacuum, with no widow status, inability to inherit or remarry without legal prosecution and being deprived of state benefits.</strong> <strong>This is the most lasting gender-based consequence of the conflict in IIOJK.</strong> <strong>This issue brief analyses the impacts of this crisis in a legal, economic, psychological and political domain; outlines the shortcomings of various policies and structures of impunity contributing to this crisis; and presents specific recommendations for international players, regional actors and civil society.</strong></p>
