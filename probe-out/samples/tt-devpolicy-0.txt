										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<p style="margin:0 0 4px;"><a href="https://devpolicy.org/one-battle-after-another-us-foreign-aid-after-the-mid-terms-20261113/"><img src="https://devpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/kaden-taylor-1U3DLk5TZE0-unsplash-768x509.jpg" alt="GAnti-Trump protest at the National Archives Museum, Washington DC, October 2025" width="600" height="398" style="display:block;max-width:100%;height:auto;border:0;" /></a></p><p style="font-size:0.85em;line-height:1.5;color:#555;margin:0 0 1.5em;font-style:italic;">Anti-Trump protest outside the National Archives, Washington DC, October 2025 (<a href="https://unsplash.com/photos/crowd-gathered-at-the-national-archives-building-1U3DLk5TZE0">Unsplash/Kaden Taylor</a>)</p><p>Less than two years after the United States Agency for International Development (USAID) <a href="https://x.com/elonmusk/status/1886307316804263979">was &#8220;fed to the wood chipper&#8221;</a> by <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk">Elon Musk</a> and tens of billions of dollars were cut from the United States&#8217; foreign aid budget, the mid-term elections have again dramatically changed the country&#8217;s political landscape. From January, the Democrats will command large majorities in the Senate and House. Two years is a long time in politics — waves come and go — but, for now, the Democrats also appear to have <a href="https://www.reuters.com/commentary/reuters-open-interest/surge-2028-us-presidential-bets-reframes-markets-after-midterms-mike-dolan-2026-11-05/">a better than even chance</a> of winning the next presidential election.</p>
<p>What will this mean for US aid? In the short term, the Democrats can now hold inquiries and hearings into <a href="https://devpolicy.org/manufactured-chaos-trump-2-0-puts-his-stamp-on-us-foreign-aid-20240129/">the chaotic demolition of USAID</a>, as well as into how a much-denuded State Department has gone about managing what is left of the US foreign aid program. In theory, given their authority over the federal budget and the fact that USAID <a href="https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10261">remains a statutorily authorised agency</a>, they could also use their legislative power to start the process of reversing some of the Trump administration&#8217;s changes. This would, however, require cooperation from the White House, which is very unlikely while Trump is its occupant.</p>
<p>If a Democrat is elected president in 2028, and the Democrats still hold the House and Senate, the odds of recovery are higher. But our advice to supporters of aid in the US is stark and simple — don&#8217;t count on it!</p>
