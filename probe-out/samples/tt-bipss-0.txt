<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Political dissent is an essential component of democratic society, yet governments increasingly frame protests, opposition movements and public grievances as threats to national security. In this commentary, BIPSS Research Assistant Farhana Rashid discusses how states use securitization as a tool of political control to delegitimise dissent, justify repression and consolidate power. Drawing on recent unrest across Europe and the July 2024 uprising in Bangladesh, she examines how legitimate political grievances can be transformed into security concerns. The commentary highlights the consequences of such practices for democratic freedoms, civil liberties and state-society relations. It also explores the importance of desecuritizing dissent through dialogue, institutional accountability and democratic engagement.</p>

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">BIPSS Research Associate Mohosina Mostofa Mity examines why countries should reconsider the assumption that they must compete in every aspect of the global AI race. The commentary argues that technological competition without strategic judgement can generate enormous financial costs, deepen inequalities, intensify security risks and divert resources from more pressing national priorities. Drawing on international experiences, it explores how strategic restraint, selective investment and cooperation can produce greater long-term value than competition alone. It also assesses Bangladesh’s AI ambitions in light of its socio-economic and institutional realities, arguing for a development approach that prioritises practical applications, human welfare and national interests over technological prestige.</p>
