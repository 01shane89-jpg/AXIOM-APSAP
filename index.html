Homes in the water

—

ProvinceSubdist.HouseholdsPeople

How to read this

Households are who lives there, not whose floor is wet. The counts are the registered households inside subdistricts where flood water is present. They are not a damage assessment, and no official per-house damage figure exists for this event yet.

The household figures never change; the footprint does. GISTDA’s registry is a fixed 2014 edition — it only moves when houses get built, so there is nothing to refresh in it. What moves hourly is which subdistricts are in the water, and that is recomputed on every refresh from the gauges that are over their bank. Treat the counts as an order of magnitude.

Two clocks, not one. The gauge-driven subdistricts are live. The satellite-derived ones are fixed at build time, because the MODIS composite is itself daily and is packaged with the page.

Gauges are the live signal. They update hourly and say exactly how far water is above or below the bank at a point. Satellite detection is daily, 250 m, and blind under cloud — absence of colour is not evidence of dry ground.

Two kinds of evidence, drawn differently. Rust subdistricts have an overflowing gauge within about 2 km. Blue ones had flood water seen from orbit. Where both apply the outline is heavier.

How to read this

This is a curated record, not a live feed. No open, keyless source publishes border incidents in real time. Each incident here was taken from a named outlet and links to it. Claims made by one army are attributed to that army.

Where the line runs is itself the dispute. The border drawn here is OpenStreetMap’s. Thailand works from a 1:50,000 map, Cambodia from the French 1:200,000 Annex I map, and several flashpoints sit in the gap between them.

Distances are measured from that line. A subdistrict counts by where its centre falls, and residents are GISTDA’s 2014 register. Read them as orders of magnitude.

Heat detections are not strikes. VIIRS picks up any fire hotter than its surroundings. In the dry season that is mostly field burning. A cluster near a flashpoint on the day of a reported clash is worth a look, but it is not confirmation.

    How to read this

    Nothing here is confirmed. Observations are instrument readings. Claims are what an agency said. Events are what a named outlet reported. Each keeps its source, and none has been reviewed by an analyst.

    A trusted source can still be wrong. Reliability describes the source; it never turns a report into a fact.

    Sources are linked, not stored. Every record links to the page it came from. Each record also carries a SHA-256 fingerprint of what this build packaged, so a changed record can be spotted.

      How alerts work

      An alert is a notice, not a finding. Firing a rule does not confirm a record or change it. Open the alert to see the record’s report.

      Each alert fires once. It is keyed to the rule, the rule’s version and the record, so a rebuild or refresh cannot create duplicates.

      Alerts show inside this page. An artifact page cannot send phone or email notifications. “New” means new since you last opened this tab in this browser.
